## The session

An executive panel on AI inside the India-EU FTA Conclave, moderated by Vivek Manoharan, with panellists from manufacturing, healthcare, IP law, enterprise SaaS and government on both the Indian and German side. Each speaker was asked for three positions: one thing they are optimistic about, one they are pessimistic about, and one they consider hype.

## What we said

**Optimistic.** AI is a force multiplier, and it levels the game. A small team ships more throughput without adding headcount, and services pricing is moving from headcount to outcomes. For MSMEs the biggest unlock is custom software: most manufacturers are stuck with rigid ERPs that never quite fit, and agentic engineering finally makes tailor-made internal tools affordable to build.

**Pessimistic.** People accelerate one step and break the rest. AI writes a thousand lines of code in minutes, which means more review, more testing and more work to ship reliably. Speed up one part of the lifecycle and you have only moved the bottleneck. Governance gets the same treatment: IP-sensitive data pasted into free chatbot accounts, no audit trail, no accountable human in the loop.

**Hype.** AGI. Foundation models are solving problems we once thought impossible, but the destination claim is not close.

## The question that stayed with us

A manufacturer asked how to connect factory machines to AI agents. The unglamorous answer is that the agent is the last step, not the first. The floor speaks Modbus, SCADA, PLC and serial ports. Before any agent does anything useful you need domain knowledge and a data engineering pipeline that acquires, transforms and makes that machine data consumable.
